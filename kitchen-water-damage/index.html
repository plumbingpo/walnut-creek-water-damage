Kitchen Water Damage: What Walnut Creek Homeowners Should Know

Count the dishwasher, disposal, drain, faucet and fridge line, and the kitchen has more places for water to connect than anywhere else in the house. Almost every one sits out of view, behind an appliance or inside a cabinet. A drip from any of them travels to the rear of the cabinet base, then spreads beneath the flooring. For homeowners in Walnut Creek, our job is to track it down, dry it out, and preserve all of the kitchen that can be saved.

The costly piece of a kitchen leak is the cabinets. Particleboard boxes swell fast and stay swollen, but solid wood and plywood frequently dry well. We pull toe kicks and drill small hidden holes to move dry air under and behind the cabinets, so in many kitchens the cabinets and countertops stay right where they are while everything dries.

Signs to Look For

  • Vinyl floor that has discolored or gone soft by the cabinets or the refrigerator
  • Side panels on the base cabinets that are flaking or swollen at the bottom
  • Opening the sink cabinet releases a stale, damp odor
  • The ceiling under the kitchen has developed a brown ring
  • Sounds of dripping or hissing with all taps closed, or an unexplained rise in the water bill
  • The toe kick boards have puffed up, turned dark, or come loose
Our Process

What Happens After You Call

1

Shut Off the Leak

The fixture or appliance line that leaked is shut off, the refrigerator or dishwasher is pulled if necessary, and we confirm the failed connection so it gets repaired before we start drying.

2

Map the Moisture

We check the cabinet bases, the wall at their back and the floor throughout the room with thermal imaging and moisture meters. Water under a kitchen floor often travels farther than expected.

3

Dry Without Demolition

We remove the toe kicks and direct air behind and beneath the cabinets. An antimicrobial is applied to damp cavities while air movers and dehumidifiers work over several days.

4

Remove Only Damage

Whatever cannot be saved, whether subfloor, flooring or part of a cabinet, comes out with care. After dry readings are confirmed, the appliances go back in and we help you arrange the remaining repairs.

How the Damage Progresses

First hour

Water Goes Underneath

A failed fitting or hose sends water under appliances and to the rear of the cabinets. Through edges and seams, it works its way under the floor covering.

24 to 48 hours

Cabinets Start Swelling

The water is absorbed by particleboard bases and toe kicks, which swell. Mold can begin in the dark, closed space under the cabinets and behind the dishwasher.

Days 3 through 7

Floors Begin Moving

Laminate swells at the edges, hardwood cups, and vinyl lifts once the adhesive under it fails. The doors and drawers on the cabinets go out of alignment.

After a week

Removal Becomes Likely

There is no restoring cabinet boxes once they have swelled. Subfloor may be delaminating, and mold behind the cabinets often means removing them along with the countertop above.

Hidden Risks

What Hides Behind It

Refrigerator Water Line

The thin tube feeding the ice maker can leak a few drops a minute. When the fridge never gets moved, this amount will rot the flooring and the wall.

Flooring Over Flooring

It is common for a kitchen floor to be laid right over the previous one. Water gets trapped between the layers, where it cannot evaporate and will not show up on the surface.

The Wall Behind the Cabinets

The wall at the back of the sink and dishwasher holds drain and supply pipes. Cabinets fully cover that drywall and framing, so a leak can soak them and mold can spread out of sight.

The Dishwasher Cavity

A slow drip at the supply or drain connection wets the subfloor and the cabinet sides next to it. Until the floor changes, the dishwasher covers everything.
