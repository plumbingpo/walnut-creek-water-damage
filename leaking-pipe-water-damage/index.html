Leaking Pipe Water Damage in Walnut Creek: What to Know

You seldom catch a pipe leak the day it begins. Water seeps out behind drywall, at the back of a sink cabinet or under the concrete until a stain, a soft floor or an unexplained water bill gives it away. Often that means weeks of moisture have already gone into whatever surrounds the pipe. Our team in Walnut Creek finds how far the moisture has spread and dries all of it, not just the part you can see.

Fixing the pipe ends the leak, yet the moisture already inside the wall or floor stays put. Getting it out is the work we do. We use moisture meters and thermal imaging to map the wet materials, open only what has to be opened, and set air movers and dehumidifiers until readings are back to normal. Because slow leaks give mold time to start, we also check for growth and treat affected surfaces.

Right Now

Your First Steps

  1. Shut Off the WaterClose the fixture's supply valve if the leak is at a sink or toilet. When the source is unclear, the house main is the valve to turn off.
  2. Keep Power AwayTurn off the breaker serving the area when water is close to a light fixture, switch or outlet. Keep your hands off cords and devices that have gotten wet.
  3. Read the MeterShut off all taps and fixtures, then go look at the water meter. If the dial is still moving, a pipe is leaking somewhere you cannot see, possibly under the slab.
  4. Clear Out and Take PhotosEmpty the closet or cabinet by the leak, then photograph the pipe, any stains and any warped material before repairs start or items are discarded.
  5. Have the Area MeteredBefore the opening is sealed, get the surrounding materials checked with a moisture meter. A wall that feels dry on the surface can still be wet inside the cavity.

Common Causes

What Causes It

  • Older copper pipe that corrodes from the inside until a pinhole opens in the wall of the line.
  • Fasteners from remodeling work or wall hangings that nick or pierce a line inside the wall.
  • Leaks at a sink drain joint or trap, which release a little water with every use.
  • Loose or worn compression fittings and supply connections under sinks and behind toilets.
  • Cold snaps that freeze piping in unheated spaces, leaving small cracks that leak after the thaw.
What Is at Risk

Where the Damage Spreads

Wet Insulation

Like a sponge, wall insulation takes in water from the pipe and keeps the framing next to it damp. Since a closed wall gives it almost no airflow, soaked insulation is normally removed.

Rotted Framing

After weeks of dampness, bottom plates and wall studs start to go soft and rot. Wood that has lost its strength has to be cut out and replaced.

Mold Inside the Wall

Mold thrives in a wall cavity that is both damp and dark. With a slow leak it often covers the back of the drywall before a single spot shows on the painted side.

Swollen Cabinets

Sink cabinet bottoms are usually particleboard, which swells and crumbles when it soaks up a leak. Because the board never shrinks back, a long leak often ends with a new cabinet base.

Coverage

What About Insurance?

Insurance treats slow leaks differently from sudden ones. Policies often cover damage that is sudden and accidental. A leak lasting weeks or months usually falls outside that, and certain policies name it as an exclusion. A hidden leak you had no way of knowing about may be handled more favorably than one that was visible and ignored. Check your policy and ask your agent. We document the source of the leak, moisture readings and photos so the adjuster has clear facts.
