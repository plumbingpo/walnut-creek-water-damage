Toilet Overflow Cleanup Help in Walnut Creek

A toilet overflow looks like a mop job, but the water does not stay on the tile. It runs under the baseboard, into the wall and through the gap around the drain pipe. Most important is what the water was carrying. Water from the tank or supply line is clean. Bowl water is not clean, so we handle it as contaminated from the start.

The first thing our Walnut Creek team does is identify the water category, since every later step depends on it. Clean water means extraction and drying. Black or gray water calls for protective equipment, taking out porous materials that got soaked, carpet pad for example, and disinfecting everything it contacted. After that, moisture meters go on the wall bases, the subfloor and the room below to make sure no wet spot remains.

First Five Minutes

What to Do Right Now

  1. Shut Off the SupplyBehind the toilet is a small valve, and turning it fully clockwise shuts the water off. When the valve is frozen, remove the lid of the tank and hold the float up so filling stops.
  2. Do Not Flush AgainHold off on flushing, and keep the washer, showers and sinks off too. With a blocked drain line, whatever goes down a drain may return through the toilet.
  3. Stay Out of Contaminated WaterBowl water, or any water with waste in it, means kids and pets stay out of the room. Should you have to cross it, wear gloves and waterproof footwear.
  4. Check the Room BelowFor an upstairs toilet, look at the ceiling underneath. Should you see drips or a sag, cut the power to the room and get belongings out from under it.
  5. Contain Clean WaterOld towels can stop clean water before it gets to a hallway or carpet. Leave contaminated water for a crew with proper equipment and disinfectants.
Insurance

What About Insurance?

Many homeowners policies cover a toilet overflow when it is sudden and accidental, such as a failed fill valve or an unexpected clog. Water that backs up from the sewer or a drain is often excluded unless you added a backup endorsement, and that can apply to an overflow caused by a blocked main line. Damage from a toilet that leaked at the base for months is usually not covered. Your agent or the policy wording can tell you which situation you are in.

The Usual Culprits

What Causes It

  • A cracked tank or failed supply line that floods the bathroom with clean water.
  • Septic systems that are full or drain fields that are waterlogged, which send water back toward the house.
  • Blockage in the main sewer line, which pushes sewage back out at the lowest toilet.
  • Roots growing into the sewer line, or a section that has collapsed, slowing all the drains.
  • Too much paper, wipes or a child's toy flushed and lodged in the trap.
What Is at Risk

Where the Damage Spreads

Subfloor Saturation

Water gets beneath tile and vinyl through grout lines and the toilet base. The plywood underneath swells, delaminates and can loosen the flooring bonded to it.

Ceiling Below

In a second story bathroom, water travels down along the drain pipe and collects on top of the ceiling drywall below, which stains, sags and in some cases collapses.

Vanity and Drywall Wicking

Water spreads to the vanity toe kick and the lower edge of the drywall, where it wicks upward. Paint may look normal even when the wall is damp several inches up from the floor.

Bacterial Contamination

Bowl and drain water leaves bacteria in carpet, pad, baseboards and drywall. Anything porous is normally removed, since it cannot be disinfected with confidence.
