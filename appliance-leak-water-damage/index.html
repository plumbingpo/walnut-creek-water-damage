Dealing With Appliance Leak Water Damage in Walnut Creek

Three appliances cause most of the leaks we see. A washing machine supply hose can split and run at full pressure until someone shuts it off. A dishwasher tends to leak slowly from a door seal or drain hose, out of sight under the cabinets. Behind a refrigerator, the thin tube feeding the ice maker may drip for months. Cleanup is different for each of them.

Our Walnut Creek crew starts by pulling the appliance out and finding where the water went. In kitchens that usually means under the cabinets and beneath the flooring. With a laundry leak we look at the rooms next to it and the wall behind the washer, plus the ceiling below when the laundry sits on an upper floor. Then our team removes the standing water, meters the materials that soaked it up, and places equipment matched to the size of the damage.

Before We Arrive

  1. Shut the Machine DownCancel the cycle, and pull the plug only when the floor and the outlet near it are dry. When either one is wet, turn the power off at the breaker.
  2. Shut Off the Water LineBehind a washer, on the wall, are separate hot and cold valves. Look below the sink for the valve that feeds a dishwasher. A refrigerator line has a small valve below or behind the unit.
  3. Soak Up Standing WaterMop or towel up what you can reach so it stops spreading under cabinets and flooring. Vacuums are safe for this only when built for wet and dry pickup.
  4. Leave the Door ShutIf the washer or dishwasher has not drained, leave it shut. Opening the door mid-cycle can release the whole load of water onto the floor.
  5. Document What FailedPhotograph the failed fitting, valve or hose, and hold on to the part itself. Both the appliance maker and your insurer may want to look at it.
Will Insurance Pay?

What About Insurance?

When a machine overflows unexpectedly or a hose bursts, that is typically treated as sudden and accidental, which many homeowners policies cover. Payment typically goes toward the damage the water did, and the appliance or the part that failed is not included. A claim is harder when an ice maker line or dishwasher seeped slowly, since coverage usually does not extend to gradual leaks. Certain insurers will want to know the hose's age and whether it was kept up. Check your policy, keep the failed part and take photos before cleanup.

Why It Happens

What Causes It

  • A dishwasher that was installed out of level or with a supply connection left loose.
  • A washer drain hose that slips out of the standpipe, or a clogged standpipe.
  • Dishwasher parts that wear out, including the door gasket, the drain hose and the seal on the pump.
  • Rubber washing machine supply hoses that weaken with age and split under pressure.
  • The water inlet valve on a washer or dishwasher failing to close, which lets the machine overfill.
What Is at Risk

What Happens if You Wait

Adjoining Rooms

Water may pour from a washer hose for hours on end. That water moves beneath walls into bedrooms, closets and hallways, soaking pad and carpet far from the laundry room.

Ceiling Below an Upstairs Laundry

In a home with an upstairs laundry, leak water passes through the floor and pools on the ceiling underneath. Drywall there sags and stains while the insulation over it holds water.

Flooring and Subfloor

Hardwood, vinyl plank and laminate all let water slip underneath. The boards swell or cup, while the subfloor below remains wet well after the top looks dry.

Cabinet Damage

Toe kicks and cabinet sides take on water from underneath when a dishwasher leaks. The panels swell and separate, and wet cabinet boxes can hold moisture against the wall for weeks.
